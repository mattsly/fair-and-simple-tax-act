**Subject:** Lifetime capital gains meets inheritance-as-income

Professor Batchelder,

I am recently (pre)retired software entrepeneur and, after experiencing the (byzantine and inequitable) tax code from many different angles, have been exploring a "second act" premised on the question: what happens if we apply product design thinking to the tax code? The project is [The Tax Refactor](https://taxrefactor.substack.com/).

The first proposal is the [Lifetime Capital Gains Framework](https://taxrefactor.substack.com/p/the-lifetime-gains-framework). Quick summary: every American gets a lifetime capital gains exemption (~$2M single, ~$4M married - placeholders); above it, rates phase up to ordinary income; realization expands to four events: sale, gift, death, and borrowing. Stepped-up basis dies, buy-borrow-die closes, and a raft of loopholes (QSBS, 121, 1031) and unnecessary complexity (AMT, NIIT) can be eliminated.

I'm based in Boston and have been comparing notes with Ray Madoff, who recommended your work. Reading "[Leveling the Playing Field](https://www.hamiltonproject.org/assets/files/Batchelder_LO_FINAL.pdf),"what struck me is the similarity in the design systems: using lifetime accounting and also mitigating wealth disparity through simplifying and condensing the tax code rather than expanding it. 

I'd value your read on the proposal if you have the time and interest? As a next step I am hoping to find a way to get it rigorously scored, and I'd appreciate your perspective on the right way to do that.

Thanks for taking the time!

Matt Sly
