Professor Batchelder,

I'm a software entrepreneur with twenty years of experience building products used by tens of millions of people. I (pre)retired from software last year and, after experiencing the (byzantine and inequitable) tax code from many different angles, have been applying my product design sensibility to it. The project is The Tax Refactor.

The first proposal is the Lifetime Capital Gains Framework. Quick summary: every American gets a lifetime capital gains exemption (~$2M single, ~$4M married); above it, rates phase up to ordinary income; and realization expands to four events: sale, gift, death, and borrowing. Stepped-up basis dies, buy-borrow-die closes, and a raft of loopholes (QSBS, 121, 1031) and unnecessary complexity (AMT, NIIT) can be eliminated.

I'm based in Boston and have been comparing notes with Ray Madoff, who recommended your work. Reading “Leveling the Playing Field,” what struck me is the similarity in the design systems: lifetime accounting, mitigating wealth disparity, and simplifying the code at the same time.

I'd value your read on the proposal. As a next step I want to get it rigorously scored, and I'd especially value your perspective on the right way to do that.

Thanks, and have a great weekend!
