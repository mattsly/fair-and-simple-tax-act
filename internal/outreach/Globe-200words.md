Re "[Is Elon really the bad guy here?](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/17/opinion/elon-musk-trillionaire-spacex-ipo-tax-rich/?p1=BGSearch_Overlay_Results)" (Opinion, June 17): Your writers debate whether to confiscate Musk's fortune or leave it alone. But a new wealth tax isn't the only way to make billionaires (and trillionaires!) pay their fair share.

The more elegant approach is to address the root cause of extreme wealth disparity: the tax treatment of capital gains.

Rather than taxing all capital gains at a low rate (currently from 15% to 23.8%) and perpetuating complex carve-outs, **capital gains should be taxed based on the lifetime total of accumulated gains**.

Consider this SpaceX example: an early career SpaceX engineer took real risks when she took a significant pay cut compared to the job she could have taken at Google. She deserves preferential tax treatment when realizing ~$1M in capital gains from this IPO. But Musk, having already achieved extreme wealth, isn't taking that same level of financial risk. Preferential capital gains treatment should phase up to the top income rate at ~$5M. **Capital gains for the already rich should be taxed just at the top income rate**. And if we also **treat death and borrowing against assets as realization events**, we close "buy, borrow, die" and the vectors for passing wealth down to heirs without paying taxes.

This approach actually **simplifies the tax code, addresses the root cause of wealth inequality and raises hundreds of billions in new tax revenue**.
