# More Redistribution, Less Government

A working set of principles for a centrist Democratic platform. The one-line pitch:
**more redistribution, less government.** The wager is that those two things only
sound contradictory. Redistribution can be delivered as cash and rules instead of
as agencies and programs — and a party that concedes government *operations* are
often bad, while insisting government *transfers* can be good, neutralizes the
"they'll just waste it" attack that kills every tax-the-rich argument.

The discipline that makes this hold together is a **limiting principle** (below).
Without it, "less government" is a vibe and "more redistribution" is a blank check.

---

## The limiting principle

Markets are the default. Government acts **only** where markets demonstrably fail,
and there are exactly three ways they fail:

1. **Externalities** — costs or benefits the market doesn't price (carbon; also a
   well-educated and healthy population, whose social returns exceed private ones).
2. **Public goods** — non-excludable, non-rival things markets won't supply
   (national defense, basic research).
3. **Concentrated power** — monopoly, monopsony, and capture that let incumbents
   extract rather than compete (non-competes, licensing cartels, regulatory moats).

Name the failure. Measure it. Use the lightest tool that fixes it. This is what
gives "smallest government" teeth — and it's why "externality" can't be a catch-all
bucket. If everything is an externality, the word disciplines nothing and the whole
frame collapses into "stuff we'd like to do."

## The seven principles

**1. Clean democracy.**
Reduce the power of money to buy outcomes — campaign finance, lobbying's revolving
door, election manipulation. *Honesty clause:* this is a valence issue (nobody runs
on more corruption) and it's awkward coming from the better-funded party. It's only
real if it names mechanisms and applies to our own money too (small-donor and union
money included). Otherwise it's wallpaper.

**2. A durable, fair fiscal path.**
Put the structural deficit on a sustainable track. The **bottom 80% come out ahead
on net** — not "pay zero new tax," but "whatever they pay back, the dividend,
healthcare savings, or check makes them whole-plus." *Why the change:* the absolute
"no new tax on the bottom 80%" can't survive contact with universal healthcare or a
carbon price, because no country funds a generous state on the rich alone (there
isn't enough flow up there). Net incidence is defensible; the gross promise isn't.

**3. Universal healthcare, lower total cost, maintained quality.**
The hard one — and we say so. Pick at most two of the three legs honestly rather
than promising all three for free. Delivered, where possible, as coverage/cash and
rules, not as a single federal operator (that's how it stays consistent with #6).

**4. Opportunity by potential, not circumstance.**
Rawls' veil of ignorance, applied hardest to the accidents of birth. "Achieve"
means a guaranteed floor at the bottom two levels of Maslow — food, shelter,
physical safety. A floor of *outcomes* there, so the *opportunity* above it is real.

**5. Sturdy wall, large gate.**
Hard to enter illegally; easy, fast, and equitable to enter legally. The best-polling
idea in the set. *Open seam (see below):* this collides with #4 and needs an explicit
answer.

**6. Smallest operational footprint that does the job.**
The real departure from the Democratic stereotype, and the engine for everything
else. Not "small government" as a number, but **deliver guarantees as cash and rules,
not bureaucracies.** A child credit through the IRS is small government; a new agency
to administer the same dollars is not. Simple tax code, streamlined delivery.

**7. Capitalism that prices what it breaks — and builds the next century.**
Pro-market, with externalities and concentrated power actually measured and
mitigated. America leads in **both AI and clean energy.** *Named conflict:* AI is
enormously energy-hungry and "lead in green" via subsidy is industrial policy
(big-government picking winners). The honest reconciliation is a massive clean-energy
buildout (nuclear, geothermal, grid) that powers the AI load — and calling managed
capitalism what it is, rather than pretending it's laissez-faire.

## What we deliberately leave out — and the one exception

The platform is near-silent on culture-war social issues. That's a choice, not an
oversight, and it's a defensible realignment bet: fight for the material floor,
otherwise leave people alone.

**The one carve-out: bodily autonomy / abortion stays loud.** It's not a liability,
it's the asset — post-Dobbs the abortion-rights side has won essentially every
statewide ballot measure, red states included. Going silent there isn't discipline,
it's disarmament, and it's likely fatal in a primary. The silence is for the genuine
50/50 flashpoints, not for the 60% issue.

Note: this does **not** "bundle into Maslow." Maslow's bottom two levels are
material; autonomy and dignity sit higher or sit outside the pyramid as rights.
Don't claim a material floor logically subsumes "marry who you love" — it doesn't.

## Open seams (unresolved)

- **#4 × #5 — the magnet problem.** A guaranteed material floor *plus* a large legal
  gate is the opposition's favorite trap: "you built a welfare magnet." Needs an
  explicit answer — does the Maslow floor apply to new arrivals, and on what
  timeline? This is the single most exploitable seam in the platform.
- **The fiscal equations still need numbers.** #2, #3, #6, #7 are mutually
  constrained. The platform asserts they balance; it hasn't yet shown the arithmetic.
  Carbon-dividend recycling and a broad consumption base are the likely load-bearing
  pieces — but that math is owed.
- **#6 × the coalition.** "Your agencies are inefficient, we'll route around them
  with checks" picks a fight with public-sector unions inside the tent. Decide
  whether that fight is the point or a cost you're not pricing.
